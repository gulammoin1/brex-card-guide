# Brex Card Guide for Startup Founders

Starting a company is hard enough without wrestling with a traditional corporate card application. Many founders discover that banks want a long credit history, a personal guarantee, and stacks of paperwork before they will issue a single card for team spending.

Brex takes a different approach. If you are comparing options, a [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card/) can be worth a close look because it was built specifically for startups and growing companies, rather than adapted from a consumer product.

The most important difference is that Brex does not require a personal guarantee. Your personal assets and personal credit score are not put on the line for company spending, which helps founders keep business and personal finances separate from day one.

Underwriting is also different. Instead of focusing mainly on a founder's personal credit history, Brex looks at your company's cash balance, funding, and spending patterns. That can help well-funded early-stage startups qualify earlier, set sensible limits, and scale those limits as the business grows.

Rewards are designed around how startups actually spend. Teams can earn points on rideshare, software subscriptions, flights, hotels, and everyday business purchases, then redeem those points for travel, cash back, or other rewards that support growth.

Practical controls matter too. Finance leads can issue virtual and physical cards for employees, set spend limits by card, track receipts in real time, and integrate expenses with accounting tools. That visibility makes month-end closing faster and reduces surprise spending.

Brex is not right for every business. Eligibility, limits, and rewards can change, and sole proprietors or very early businesses may face different requirements, so check the current terms carefully before you apply.

Used thoughtfully, the [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card/) can give founders a cleaner way to manage team spending, protect personal credit, and earn rewards on the costs they already have.
